## What you're actually buying

Shiba Inu launched in August 2020, created by an anonymous developer using the name Ryoshi as an alternative to Dogecoin. It's an ERC-20 token on Ethereum, which matters more than it sounds — it means SHIB can sit in any wallet that handles Ethereum tokens, and it can be traded on nearly every major centralized exchange.

The supply number is the thing people underestimate. SHIB started with one quadrillion tokens. Ryoshi locked half of that into Uniswap liquidity and sent the other 500 trillion to Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin, who burned roughly 90% of what he received and donated the remainder to India's COVID relief fund. Even after that, around 589 trillion SHIB are still in circulation.

That's why one token costs a fraction of a cent, and why "SHIB will hit $1" talk usually ignores how much supply would have to disappear for that to be mathematically possible. The token has also grown an actual ecosystem beyond the joke: ShibaSwap, the LEASH and BONE tokens, the Shiboshi NFT collection (10,000 pieces, sold out in about 34 minutes), the Shiba Eternity card game, and the Shibarium layer-2 network.

None of that makes it a safe asset. More on that below.

## P2P, card, or spot market: which one to use

Pick based on what you actually have.

**You have crypto already** → on-chain deposit, then buy SHIB on the spot market. Cheapest path, no fiat conversion, no card processor taking a cut.

**You have local currency in a bank account** → P2P or a bank transfer. P2P charges no taker fee on Gate's side and settles with escrow, but remember the 24-hour lock on coins bought that way. Bank transfer avoids percentage fees but takes days.

**You want SHIB in the next five minutes and don't care about 2%** → card purchase. It's the most expensive route and the fastest, and those two facts are related.

**You want a small amount without the order book** → Convert. No fee, instant, with a per-trade minimum shown in the interface.

## Where to keep it afterwards

Exchange custody is fine for money you're actively trading and a poor choice for money you plan to sit on for years. Gate's own security guidance points the same direction: enable 2FA through an authenticator app, separate your login password from your trading and withdrawal passwords, whitelist withdrawal addresses, and don't create API keys with withdrawal permissions unless your strategy genuinely requires it.

For long-term holdings, move SHIB to a self-custody wallet. Because it's an ERC-20 token, any Ethereum-compatible wallet works — software wallets for convenience, hardware wallets for something you intend to leave alone.

## The risk side, stated plainly

SHIB is a meme coin. It has a large community and a real product ecosystem, and it also has no cash flows, no earnings, and no fundamentals in the traditional sense. Its price moves on sentiment, social media attention, and whatever a high-profile account posts that day. It peaked in 2021 and still trades far below that level.

This isn't a fringe view. Forbes Advisor quoted Daniel Polotsky, co-founder and chairman of crypto ATM operator CoinFlip, saying: "The largest drawback to investing in Shiba Inu is that it is very risky, even more so than most of its crypto counterparts." Madeline Hume, a senior research analyst at Morningstar, went further: "We wouldn't consider a purchase of Shiba Inu to be an investment. Shiba Inu is a meme coin, which means it derives its value from other investors' enthusiasm for the coin rather than any fundamentals."

Two practical consequences. First, position sizing matters more here than with almost any other asset — money you'd be annoyed to lose, not money you need. Second, meme coin markets move as a group, so when capital rotates into a newer token, SHIB often drops alongside everything else in the category regardless of what its own ecosystem is doing.

The general rug-pull warning applies too, though less to SHIB itself than to tokens copying its branding. Anonymous teams, locked selling, and guaranteed-return promises are the standard warning signs.
